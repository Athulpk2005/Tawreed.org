# UAE regulatory research for expanded Tawreed website

Research date: 15 October 2026. Primary government/regulator sources only support the recommendations below. This is a content evidence register, not an assertion that Tawreed holds a particular licence, certification, facility or system. English translations should be read with the governing Arabic instruments and amendments for a live matter.

## Critical 2026 WPS change

The UAE Government portal, updated 5 June 2026, expressly identifies **Ministerial Resolution No. 340 of 2026** as the current WPS instrument and states that wages for the preceding month are due on the first day of each Gregorian month. Do not reproduce the old 15-day grace-period rule or the former 80% threshold from 2022 materials as current policy. The primary PDF could not be fetched in this research session, so avoid elaborating detailed enforcement days or numerical payment thresholds from secondary reports. A statutory monitoring threshold never authorises underpaying contractual wages.

## WPS process mechanics

CBUAE describes UAEWPS as the mechanism for timely wage payment, employer-to-contracted-agent information flow, securing employer funds, disbursement confirmations, refund processing and regulatory data feeds. Its Rulebook expressly refers to accepted Salary Information Files (SIF) and timely payment acknowledgement. These support detailed process copy about master-data validation, approved inputs, control totals, SIF generation, approved bank/exchange/payment-agent submission and rejected-payment reconciliation. File version and agent requirements must be confirmed before processing.

## Agency / EOR model and allocation of duties

Article 9 of Cabinet Resolution No. 1 of 2022 distinguishes **intermediation** (matching without the agency becoming the employer) from **temporary employment and outsourcing** (the worker’s employment relationship is with the supplying agency and the beneficiary supervises the assignment). Agency activity requires appropriate licensing and a beneficiary agreement. The agency remains accountable for its workers and must report known beneficiary breaches of worker rights, health or safety. This is not a statutory basis for “zero liability”, unrestricted visa hosting or exemption from client worksite duties.

- Executive regulations: https://www.uaelegislation.gov.ae/en/legislations/1547/download
- Official legislation detail: https://uaelegislation.gov.ae/en/legislations/1547
- Official agency licence service: https://taqyeem.mohre.gov.ae/en/services/renewal-of-recruitmenttemporary-employment-and-mediation-agency-license-2022

Recommended wording: “The employment model, sponsoring entity, jurisdiction, work-permit route and division of employer and client duties are confirmed before an assignment is accepted. The agreement allocates payroll, benefits, supervision, worksite safety, reporting and termination responsibilities. EOR can reduce the administrative burden of establishing a direct employment infrastructure, subject to the lawful licence and permit route; it does not remove all client legal or commercial obligations.” Do not imply a mainland employer may automatically deploy free-zone-sponsored personnel anywhere or that an EOR arrangement determines corporate tax/permanent-establishment treatment.

## Federal labour-law safeguards and benefits

Federal Decree-Law No. 33 of 2021 (as amended): recruitment/employment costs cannot be charged to workers (Article 6); worker official documents must not be withheld (Article 13); worker records require retention for at least two years after service ends (Article 13); employer accommodation, safety and medical-care duties apply. Article 51’s traditional gratuity covers qualifying foreign full-time employees after one continuous year: 21 days’ basic wage for each of the first five years and 30 days thereafter, pro-rated qualifying fractions, unpaid absence excluded, capped at two years’ wage. Article 53 generally requires final wages and entitlements within 14 days of contract end. National employees require pension/social-security treatment. DIFC/ADGM need separate analysis.

- Consolidated law: https://uaelegislation.gov.ae/en/legislations/1541/download
- Official detail: https://uaelegislation.gov.ae/en/legislations/1541

Annual leave is 30 days after a year; where service exceeds six months but is less than a year, two days per month. Part-time treatment follows actual hours and the applicable regulations. Leave taken with pay and cash payment of untaken leave on exit have different calculation bases; do not collapse them into an indiscriminate “gross salary accrual” formula.

- Annual leave official guide: https://u.ae/en/information-and-services/jobs/employment-in-the-private-sector/types-of-leaves-and-entitlements-in-the-private-sector/annual-leave

Do not call accrual an escrow. A ring-fenced funded mechanism requires an actual documented arrangement. The voluntary federal Savings Scheme is distinct from traditional gratuity and uses approved investment funds; MoHRE-hosted provider guidance states basic employer contributions of 5.83% / 8.33% of monthly basic pay according to service length. Do not claim Tawreed enrolment or investment-fund authority.

- MoHRE-hosted Savings Scheme guidance: https://www.mohre.gov.ae/assets/download/885a7cb8/national-bonds-savings-scheme-customer-guide-en.aspx
- Government benefits overview: https://u.ae/en/information-and-services/jobs/Sector-of-employment/employment-in-the-private-sector/end-of-service-benefits-for-employees-in-the-private-sector

## Emiratisation / Nafis current position

MoHRE’s 22 June 2026 statement published by WAM confirms covered private-sector establishments with **50 or more workers** must increase Emiratisation in **skilled jobs** by two percentage points annually, split 1% per half-year. It confirms **AED 10,000 per month per unfilled Emirati position from 1 July 2026** (AED 120,000 annual equivalent). Do not describe this as a universal one-off fine. The cumulative programme objective reaches 10% by end-2026 for establishments following the programme trajectory; determine the actual required count in the establishment’s MoHRE record rather than invent a rounding rule. Nafis is a talent/support programme, not certification of an agency’s partnership status.

- Current MoHRE statement via WAM: https://www.wam.ae/en/article/c0uqocb-mohre-reaffirms-june-deadline-for-private-sector
- MoHRE employer guide (2% / 10% context): https://www.mohre.gov.ae/assets/download/3221206e/Awareness%20Guide%20for%20New%20Employers%20Companies%20-%20EN_639011571513592816.pdf.aspx

Selected establishments with **20–49 employees** were required to appoint one Emirati in 2024 and an additional one in 2025. Ministerial Resolution 455/2023 states AED 96,000 for an unfilled 2024 position from January 2025 and AED 108,000 for an unfilled 2025 position from January 2026, and a two-month replacement period if national headcount falls. These are dated obligations, not a verified new blanket 2026 target for every small employer.

## Insurance and welfare scope

Dubai Law11/2013 Articles9–10 makes the employer responsible for enrolling employees, funding their coverage without passing cost to them, maintaining valid insurance and producing policy evidence for residence issuance/renewal. Abu Dhabi is governed by Law23/2005 and DoH requirements. These are different systems; use “the applicable DHA, DoH or Northern Emirates framework,” not universal dual DHA/DoH certification.

- Dubai primary law: https://dlp.dubai.gov.ae/Legislation%20Reference/2013/Law%20No.%20%2811%29%20of%202013.html
- Abu Dhabi primary scheme: https://www.doh.gov.ae/-/media/09AEC8C5B6D34AA88DE110F63641B863.ashx
- Abu Dhabi primary law: https://www.doh.gov.ae/-/media/0BE585B5E6814D81913697DD6E644C02.ashx

From1 January2025 the mandatory scheme extended to private-sector employees and domestic workers in Sharjah, Ajman, Fujairah, Ras Al Khaimah and Umm Al Quwain. Insurance is required for residence issuance/renewal; previously valid permits are addressed at renewal. Employer financial obligations remain. Coverage, network, exclusions and co-payments depend on the chosen policy; annual screening and 24/7 multilingual support are service design elements, not universal statutory benefit promises.

- MoHRE basic insurance scheme: https://tasheel.mohre.gov.ae/en/media-center/awareness-and-guidance/health-insurance-scheme.aspx

Facilities, fleets, certified tracking, camp approval status, room sizes, 24/7 helpline staffing and occupational-health services require separate evidence. Describe pre-deployment accommodation/transport/HSE due diligence, named responsibility, inspection evidence and corrective action where supplied, not ownership of unverified camps/fleets. Avoid implying stock photography depicts Tawreed facilities. Replace “passport withdrawal requests” with “personal-document assistance” or “return of any original document temporarily provided for an authorised administrative process”; employees should retain their own documents.

## Data privacy and portal security

PDPL45/2021 establishes lawful, fair and transparent processing, purpose limitation, minimisation, accuracy, security and retention controls. Article4 includes specific lawful employment/contract/legal-obligation exceptions to consent; therefore do not say all HR processing always requires consent. Controller/processor roles, access restrictions, secure transfer, record retention and data-subject requests should be contractually allocated. Cross-border transfers require applicable safeguards. DIFC/ADGM and sector-specific regimes may differ.

- Primary PDPL: https://www.uaelegislation.gov.ae/en/legislations/1972/download
- Government overview: https://u.ae/en/about-the-uae/digital-uae/data/data-protection-laws.

Do not claim “256-bit SSL”, live encryption, ISO27001 hosting, biometric integrations or PDPL compliance of the preview. These require actual deployment/security evidence. Recommended preview wording: “Access is provided only after organisational onboarding and role authorisation. The live implementation must apply encrypted transport, approved identity controls, restricted permissions, logging, retention rules and the applicable UAE personal-data requirements. This preview is not connected to an authentication or payroll system.” ISO standards can be described as governance references/readiness themes; certification requires a current certificate, issuing body, scope, entity and dates.
