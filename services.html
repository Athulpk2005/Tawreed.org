SERVICE SCOPE

White-collar staffing, permanent recruitment and executive search.

Direct recruitment supports organisations that need to build internal capability through permanent appointments. The commercial case is stronger when the hiring brief describes business outcomes, competence, reporting relationships and an approved compensation range. The client remains employer and makes the final appointment; Tawreed supports the evidence and execution of the search rather than treating a CV submission as a completed service.

Coverage can extend from specialists and managers to corporate and technical appointments. Our direct recruitment approach incorporates technical and IT staffing expertise and a specialist talent database; role requirements may arise in IT, oil and gas, ports and maritime without defining the limits of the service. Candidate availability, credentials, notice periods and compensation are reconfirmed for the live mandate instead of being inferred from a historic database record.

Headhunting is appropriate when the role is strategically important, market access is restricted or an open advertisement would create commercial or organisational risk. The mandate defines the leadership challenge, decision authority, succession context and measurable outcomes for the first year. A retained, exclusive or contingency structure is agreed on the merits of the search, including milestones, conflicts, off-limits restrictions and ownership of candidate introductions.

Temporary and contract staffing supports planned peaks, employee absence, a defined project, an uncertain start-up period or repeat on-demand requirements. The objective is to align the capacity and employment arrangement with a finite operating need. The assignment brief identifies the duration, normal hours, shifts, location, supervisor, essential competencies, approved rate and the conditions for extension or early release.

SERVICE SCOPE

Industrial, logistics and blue-collar manpower deputation.

Managed manpower outsourcing is suitable when delivery requires coordinated teams, multiple locations, staggered starts or recurring administration beyond individual recruitment. The commercial brief converts workload into an approved role mix, headcount curve, shift pattern and supervision model. The service may support warehousing, facilities management, construction or EPC work, retail operations and aviation services, but it is designed around task competence and operating conditions rather than a fixed industry boundary.

The statement of work defines sourcing, trade or task testing, lawful quota and permit checks, bilingual employment documentation where required, medical fitness, Emirates ID dependencies, insurance and client access. Accommodation, meals, daily transport, uniforms, PPE or on-site coordination are included only where specified and evidenced. The mobilisation tracker identifies who provides each item and the acceptance test before the person joins the roster.

Approved channel selection is followed by identity checks, trade testing or work samples, structured interviews and any essential licence verification. Recruitment partners are reviewed for authorisation, worker-fee practices, communication accuracy and handling of original documents. The deployment list records assessment outcomes, accepted assignment terms and candidate availability. Client-requested substitutes go through equivalent checks; unsuitable or unauthorised work cannot be solved by simply adding a name to a roster.

SERVICE SCOPE

Facilities management and hospitality manpower.

Facilities and hospitality requirements are assessed by task, site, shift pattern and service standard. The brief identifies whether the client needs temporary absence cover, a recurring call-off team, an event-related increase or a longer managed assignment. Role suitability, availability, work conditions, client supervision and lawful employment arrangements are confirmed before the deployment plan is accepted.

Selection combines structured interviews and practical assessment relevant to the work: service communication, following safe procedures, hygiene, equipment handling or supervisory judgement. Required qualifications or food-handling permissions are verified where applicable. A candidate’s assignment record includes material limitations, accepted hours, induction and access dependencies so a person is not treated as ready solely because a uniform or transport seat is available.

The account plan assigns roster ownership, time approval, overtime authorisation, employee contact, PPE or uniforms, transport where included and a continuity route. Absence, performance concerns, guest-service incidents and changing capacity are handled through an accountable case and change process. The detailed service page explains the regulatory checks, local planning window, relief arrangements and post-assignment review.

SERVICE SCOPE

Volume mobilisation and international talent corridors.

Volume mobilisation begins with the approved role and cohort plan, including headcount by phase, competence, compensation, work location, shift and target release. Potential corridors include India, Pakistan, Nepal, the Philippines and appropriate East African markets through verified, authorised partners. Partner selection and source-country compliance are assessed for the live requirement; the service does not rely on an assumed overseas office or a country-independent visa timeline.

A person-level tracker follows screened, assessed, selected, offer-accepted, authorised, travel-ready, arrived and site-accepted status. Practical testing, credential review, accurate job terms, medical and identity requirements, pre-departure orientation and arrival contacts feed the readiness gates. Worker-fee concerns, misleading offers or document-retention allegations are escalated independently of the mobilisation target.

An 18–25-working-day overseas planning window is agreed only after the source route, documentation, authority dependencies and travel conditions are reviewed. The account team reports the actual critical path and releases cohorts when each worker is ready. The full international-mobilisation page details partner due diligence, country-specific checks, six-stage deployment, contingency and handover.

Six-stage deployment methodology

A release path from sourcing to site induction.

This pathway governs a worker deployment. For a permanent appointment, the client becomes the employer and owns its contract, work-authorisation and onboarding steps; the recruitment handover records the relevant joining dependencies.

  1. 01

    Sourcing

    Approve the role mix, outcomes, compensation, source channels and candidate communication before outreach.

  2. 02

    Technical testing & assessment

    Use job-relevant trade tests, work samples, technical scenarios or approved psychometric tools with a predefined scoring rubric.

  3. 03

    Compliance pre-screening

    Check identity, qualifications, eligibility, professional permission, assignment acceptance and a viable employment route.

  4. 04

    Contract formalisation

    Reconcile the offer, bilingual MoHRE documentation where required, approved terms and the legal employing arrangement.

  5. 05

    Medical & biometrics

    Complete the applicable medical, Emirates ID, insurance and authority dependencies; record status and exceptions at person level.

  6. 06

    Site induction & release

    Confirm safety training, work instructions, PPE, access, supervisor and included support arrangements before deployment.

Engagement models

Flexible scope. Clear accountability.

Can a requirement be temporary or on demand?

Yes. Scope the assignment duration, skills, volume and location, then agree availability confirmation, call-off rules, employment requirements and readiness before deployment.

Is the service restricted to a specific industry?

No industry restriction is presented. Feasibility depends on the work, permitted activities, competence, jurisdiction and safe deployment conditions.

Can one brief involve several roles and locations?

Yes. The delivery plan can coordinate role groups, local handovers, shifts and phased milestones, with responsibilities and acceptance criteria agreed in writing.
